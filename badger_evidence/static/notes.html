Black-box data: should biology generate data for models, not people?

Naef & Bronstein, Black-box data: a new paradigm for biomedicine in the AI era, Chem. Sci., 2026, 17, 8327–8344.

The claim
The Bitter Lesson already beat hand-crafted features in algorithms; the authors say data should follow. Design experiments for models to read, and trade human interpretability for 100–1000× more throughput.
Why it matters
Biology needs "the next PDB" more than "the next AlphaFold". The Protein Data Bank took about 50 years and up to $50B; no other area of biology has an equivalent training set.
My read
A useful taxonomy wrapped in advocacy. The authors hold equity in a company built on this bet, "more noisy data wins" assumes random noise when lab noise is mostly systematic, and clean data is still needed to evaluate anything.
For this project
Our own lab-to-lab noise floor (0.71 log units) is exactly that systematic noise. Bias is a design problem, solved with shared controls and forced overlap across labs, not something a model learns away afterwards.
  • Low resolutionSparse, cheap readouts; the model fills in the rest.
  • Amplifiable signalDNA barcodes let a million variants share one tube.
  • Latent pretrainingAbundant indirect data first, scarce labels second.
  • Hypothesis-freeRandom screens for coverage, not expert picks.
  • Proxy readoutsCells in a dish standing in for animals or people.
  • Skip cleaningTest crude mixtures; the model absorbs the noise.
  • Missing dataAbsences as signal, like implicit feedback.
